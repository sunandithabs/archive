AkiraRansomware Group

Ransomware · RaaS March 2023 to present

Overview

Summary

Akira has been active since March 2023. You'll see it referred to by several names depending on which vendor is writing: MITRE's own ATT&CK entry for Akira (G1024) lists Howling Scorpius, Punk Spider, and Gold Sahara as aliases of the same tracked group, not separate partners. They're Unit 42's, CrowdStrike's, and Secureworks' individual house names for the same activity cluster. CISA's advisories also reference Storm-1567, likely Microsoft's equivalent designation.

CISA notes possible overlap with the defunct Conti ransomware group. That's a different picture than the Snatch/BlackByte connection in source notes, which points more toward shared personnel than shared branding.

Akira primarily hits small and medium-sized businesses but has also hit larger organizations, and per the most recent joint advisory (November 2025) has collected roughly $244 million in ransom payments since 2023.

Victimology

Source notes place the geographic focus on the US, Canada, and Germany; CISA's broader tracking adds Europe and Australia. Sector targeting centers on critical manufacturing, education, IT, healthcare, financial services, and food and agriculture, with a clear preference for SMBs, though larger organizations get hit too. At 1,502 victims in source notes versus roughly 250 confirmed by CISA, the two numbers likely aren't measuring the same thing: source notes probably reflect leak-site claims, while CISA's figure reflects independently verified incidents.

Tradecraft: Initial Access & Credential Access

Brute-force attempts against Cisco infrastructure (T1110), exploiting CVE-2019-6693 and CVE-2022-40684 per source notes. CISA advisories add: VPN access without MFA, additional Cisco vulnerabilities (CVE-2020-3259, CVE-2023-20269), a SonicWall vulnerability (CVE-2024-40766), and credential theft via spearphishing, consistent with T1190 and T1078. Domain account creation paired with credential harvesting using Mimikatz and LaZagne (CISA), mapping to OS Credential Dumping (T1003).

Tradecraft: Persistence & C2

New domain accounts created post-compromise (T1136.002). Legitimate remote access tools, AnyDesk and LogMeIn, used to maintain access while blending into normal admin activity (T1219).

Tradecraft: Impact

Two encryptors confirmed: the original C++ build (.akira extension) and the Rust-based Megazord build (.powerranges extension), using a hybrid ChaCha20 stream cipher with RSA key exchange (CISA), mapping to T1486. Runs a double-extortion model combining encryption with leak-site publication threats, including phone calls to victim organizations to apply pressure, per FBI reporting cited by CISA.

analyst notes

The "four affiliate groups" thing confused me at first, until I looked into how each name actually gets used. MITRE's own ATT&CK page lists Howling Scorpius, Punk Spider, and Gold Sahara as aliases for the same group, not three separate partners, they're just what Unit 42, CrowdStrike, and Secureworks each call the same activity cluster. That was a useful lesson for me going in: different vendors often name the same thing differently, so a list of "associated groups" isn't always what it looks like at first glance. Once that's set aside, the connections that seem to actually mean something are the Snatch/BlackByte overlap in source notes and the suspected Conti link, both of which read as shared people rather than shared branding, though I'd want a more experienced read on that before treating it as settled.

I also noticed Akira has gone three-plus years without a rebrand, which struck me as long compared to other groups I've come across. I don't know enough yet to say this with much confidence, but one guess is that sticking mostly to small and mid-sized businesses, rather than big, headline-grabbing targets, keeps them under the kind of pressure that took down groups like LockBit. The $244 million total looks less like a few huge scores and more like a lot of smaller ones adding up over time.

The other detail that stuck with me: in June 2025, Akira reportedly hit Nutanix AHV for the first time, after already covering VMware ESXi and Hyper-V. That suggests they're paying attention to which virtualization platforms are actually in use rather than assuming everyone runs VMware. Small thing, but it's the kind of detail I want to get better at noticing on my own.
