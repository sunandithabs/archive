TheGentlemenRansomware Group

Ransomware · RaaS July 2025 to present

Overview

TheGentlemen showed its earliest development activity in July 2025, with active campaigns beginning that August. Halcyon assesses the group as a likely splinter of the Qilin ransomware-as-a-service operation, formed after a reported payment dispute, though ASEC's own research flags that lineage as unconfirmed elsewhere. The group is suspected to run out of Russian-speaking regions and, like many RaaS crews, enforces an internal ban on targeting Russia and CIS countries.

Growth has been fast: from about 27 victims across 17 countries in its first month of confirmed campaigns to nearly 300 victims across 66+ countries and 20+ industries by mid-2026 per Halcyon's tracking, against the 442-victim figure in current source notes. Halcyon describes it as one of the fastest-scaling ransomware operations currently being tracked.

Thailand tops the victim list; Halcyon puts the US share at only ~7%, well below the ~50% typical of the broader ransomware ecosystem. Manufacturing and construction lead sector targeting, per Ankura Consulting, due to low downtime tolerance and high-value data.

Source notes describe valid account abuse (T1078), spearphishing attachment delivery (T1566.001), and vishing (T1566.004); a four-technique total was referenced but only three were detailed. External reporting fills in a likely fourth: exploitation of internet-facing infrastructure, most notably FortiGate appliances via CVE-2024-55591, a FortiOS/FortiProxy auth bypass (T1190), per Surefire Cyber. Reporting also notes use of infostealer malware and initial access brokers to source credentials ahead of intrusion.

Registry run keys (T1547.001) per source notes. Privilege escalation via exploitation (T1068); specific vulnerability not identified in current sourcing.

PowerShell (T1059.001), WMI (T1059.005 per source mapping), Python (T1059.006). Trend Micro documents abuse of legitimate signed drivers and custom anti-AV utilities to disable endpoint protection (T1562.001), alongside GPO manipulation to push payloads domain-wide (T1484.001).

GPO-based payload distribution via NETLOGON shares (T1021.002), enabling near-simultaneous infection across domain-joined machines. Runs a double-extortion model: data exfiltrated over encrypted channels before encryption, with publication threatened on a leak site if the ransom isn't paid (ASEC, Trend Micro). Exact exfiltration protocol isn't detailed in current sourcing.

analyst notes

Something that jumped out while putting this together: most of the tradecraft here scales without extra work per victim, GPO abuse and NETLOGON propagation hit every machine on a domain at once, and the credentials reportedly come from infostealers and brokers rather than the group's own phishing effort. Vishing doesn't fit that pattern, it takes an actual phone call per attempt and doesn't scale the same way. I don't have enough to prove this, but my guess is that the undocumented fourth initial access technique in the source notes isn't another mass-market option, it's probably a fallback used when the cheap stuff doesn't work, something like calling a help desk after phishing fails. Take that as a hunch, not a finding.

The geography also surprised me, I'd assumed most ransomware victims were US-based. Brazil and India showing up more than expected too. Not sure what to make of it beyond flagging it, if the Qilin-splinter theory holds, it might just reflect where those affiliates were already operating.

One thing I'm trying to keep in mind while reading these reports: "first observed" just means when defenders started watching, not when the people behind a group learned their craft. A name barely a year old pulling off domain-wide GPO deployment and purpose-built anti-AV tooling on day one probably isn't run by beginners, even if the brand itself is.
